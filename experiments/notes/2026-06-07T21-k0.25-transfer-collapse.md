fitness improvement over 0.104.

Mechanism theory: lower K → less phase pulling → more phase diversity → more "transparent"
to the 0.5 Hz drive → carrier builds more coherently. The A=0.15 amplitude scaffolding
prevents the xi collapse that appeared at K=0.5+A=0.1 (T10, t2 xi=0.548).

## Analysis

### Transfer collapse is the dominant failure

transfer_score = 0.323 at K=0.25 vs 0.655 at K=0.5 (−0.332, or −51%). This is a
deterministic, total structural failure. At transfer weight 0.15:

```
(1 − 0.323) × 0.15 = 0.102
```

That single term alone nearly equals the entire baseline fitness (0.104). No improvement
in carrier_e or xi can compensate.

### Carrier_e did NOT extend beyond K=0.5

The prediction that carrier_e would continue rising below K=0.5 was falsified:
- K=0.5, A=0.15: carrier_e = 0.853 (T11)
- K=0.25, A=0.15: carrier_e = 0.848 (this fire, −0.005)

The carrier_e trend does not monotonically extend below K=0.5. The K=0.5 operating
point appears to be near a local maximum for carrier_e at this drive amplitude.

### K=0.5 is a critical threshold for transfer

The K gradient at A=0.15 now has three data points:
- K=1.0: transfer avg 0.694
- K=0.5: transfer avg 0.655
- K=0.25: transfer avg 0.323 (deterministic)

The collapse from K=0.5 to K=0.25 is not a smooth gradient — it's a phase transition.
At K=0.5, weak Kuramoto coupling still creates enough category-cluster coherence for
the B-engine to discriminate primed vs naive memories. At K=0.25, the coupling is
below the effective synchronization threshold: category structure fails to form, and
transfer collapses.

This is consistent with Kuramoto theory: for a network of N oscillators with coupling K,
synchronization onset occurs at a critical K_c that scales with the frequency distribution
width. Below K_c, global synchrony is impossible and even partial clustering fails.
K=0.5 sits just above the effective K_c for transfer; K=0.25 sits below it.

### magic_R non-monotonicity: drive-dominated phase dynamics

At K=0.25, magic_R = 0.291 — HIGHER than K=1.0's 0.250 and far above K=0.5's 0.161.
The expected monotonic decrease (lower K → more phase diversity → lower R) is violated.

Proposed mechanism: at K=0.25, Kuramoto coupling is too weak to counteract phase
dynamics driven by the A=0.15 amplitude modulation. The strong drive at 0.5 Hz
imposes its own phase entrainment on memories, creating amplitude-correlated phase
clustering that raises R independently of K. The result is a "drive-dominated" phase
state — higher R but no transfer-useful category structure.

This distinguishes two sources of phase order:
1. Kuramoto-induced: forms category clusters → enables transfer
2. Drive-induced (at very low K): produces global phase entrainment → raises R but
   does not form transfer-useful structure

At K=0.5 and above, Kuramoto coupling is strong enough to dominate and form category
clusters. At K=0.25, drive dominates and produces a globally entrained but
category-incoherent phase state.

### xi_robustness is high but stochastic

xi avg = 0.888 across 2 trials (0.959, 0.816). This is actually higher than K=0.5+A=0.15
(0.873 avg in T11), consistent with the A=0.15 scaffolding hypothesis — but the
improvement is noise-level and fitness is dominated by the transfer collapse.

## Implications

1. **K=0.5 is at or near a synchronization threshold for transfer.** The transfer_score
   transition from 0.655 (K=0.5) to 0.323 (K=0.25) is sharp and deterministic — a
   structural phase transition, not a smooth gradient. No intermediate K (e.g., K=0.35)
   is likely to recover the full transfer value; it would sit in the transition zone.

2. **Carrier_e saturates at K=0.5 for this drive.** The downward K trend does not
   extend carrier_e further below K=0.5 at A=0.15. The 0.853 carrier_e at K=0.5 is
   near the stage_sync ceiling for this drive regime.

3. **magic_R non-monotonicity reveals two distinct phase-ordering mechanisms:**
   - Kuramoto-dominated (K ≥ 0.5): R ≤ 0.25, transfer works
   - Drive-dominated (K ≤ 0.25): R ≈ 0.29, transfer collapses
   These are qualitatively different dynamical regimes. The R-transfer relationship is
   regime-specific: within Kuramoto-dominated regime, lower K → lower R → better xi
   (T10/T11 pattern); but crossing into drive-dominated regime breaks this rule.

4. **irx remains the best configuration overall.** Stage_sync is bounded above by
   carrier_e=0.853, transfer avg=0.655 (K=0.5 ceiling). The irx mode (A=0.1, 0.5Hz)
   achieves carrier_e=0.935, transfer=0.836, fitness avg=0.099. The remaining headroom
   in stage_sync is marginal.

5. **Future stage_sync exploration should stay K ≥ 0.5.** Sub-0.5 K values will
   reliably collapse transfer. The only remaining stage_sync direction is finding
   configurations that improve transfer (currently bounded at 0.655) without losing
   carrier_e.
