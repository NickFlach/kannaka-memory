## Hypothesis

At 1.0 Hz, the drive completes two full cycles over the 16-cycle dream window:
- Arc 1 positive: cycles 0–2 (peak at cycle 2, t=0.25 s)
- Arc 1 negative: cycles 2–6 (trough at cycle 4)
- Arc 2 positive: cycles 6–10 (peak at cycle 8)
- Arc 2 negative: cycles 10–14 (trough at cycle 12)
- Arc 3 positive: cycles 14–16 (rising)

**Prediction:** Two refine phases (negative arcs at cycles 2–6 and 10–14) double the
carrier sharpening relative to 0.5 Hz's single negative arc. The carrier_emergence
metric measures [0.5, 4.0] Hz band — 1.0 Hz lands well inside. irx phase relaxation
is geometry-driven and should be frequency-neutral. Expected: carrier_e ≥ 0.9,
xi similar to baseline (~0.55), fitness ≤ 0.099.

**Alternative (two-arc cancellation):** The alternating positive/negative pattern
means each memory spends roughly equal time boosted and suppressed, reducing net
amplitude differential. The 0.5 Hz advantage comes from the asymmetric single arc
(large positive, small negative), not from the number of arcs. Two symmetric arcs
cancel the net effect.

## Analysis

### Two-arc cancellation confirmed

At 0.5 Hz, the single positive arc (cycles 0–8) provides an asymmetric boost: strong
early enhancement, then the gentle negative arc (9–16) creates amplitude contrast by
selectively trimming weaker associations below the FFT detection threshold. The NET
amplitude differential is large and unidirectional.

At 1.0 Hz, memory amplitudes are pushed positive then negative then positive then
negative in rapid succession. Each memory spends approximately equal time in boosted
and suppressed phases (two full cycles). The net amplitude differential approaches zero.
The amplitude time series DOES oscillate at 1.0 Hz — carrier_e registers 0.661 (not
zero), capturing the periodicity in [0.5, 4.0] Hz. But the PHYSICAL carrier structure
(consistent amplitude separation between carrier and non-carrier memories) is much
weaker than at 0.5 Hz.

**Transfer collapses (0.688 vs 0.836):** Transfer score measures B-engine amplitude
discrimination after priming. The priming advantage depends on carrier amplitude
differentiation established by the drive. At 1.0 Hz, the weaker net differential
means the primed B-engine has less amplitude structure to exploit, reducing the
primed-vs-naive discrimination.

**carrier_e intermediate (0.661, not zero):** Unlike 0.25 Hz (carrier_e=0.638 from T22),
which removed the negative arc entirely, 1.0 Hz still has negative arcs. The FFT peak
at 1.0 Hz is measurable but the amplitude envelope doesn't differentiate memories
cleanly, so the peak-to-total ratio is lower than 0.5 Hz.

**xi unchanged (0.540 ≈ 0.559 baseline):** xi is determined by the constructive
interference geometry, not the drive frequency. The irx phase relaxation runs after the
drive and uses phase-similarity neighbors. Frequency variation doesn't affect the phase
neighborhood structure, so xi is frequency-neutral (as predicted).

**magic_R and query_gravity invariant (0.617, 0.363):** Both are determined by
post-dream phase distribution geometry, which depends on irx's constructive-pair
relaxation, not on the drive frequency. These are structural properties of irx mode.

### Why the first peak timing alone is insufficient

T00 suggested that the 0.5 Hz benefit comes from "early peak at cycle 4 → 12 cycles
of post-peak consolidation." At 1.0 Hz, the first peak is at cycle 2 (14 cycles of
post-peak). But the T00 analysis was for stage_sync where consolidation integrates
over remaining cycles. Under irx, the phase relaxation runs ONCE at the end of the
dream chain, not integrated over cycles. The "consolidation time after peak" concept
doesn't apply to irx the same way.

The actual mechanism for 0.5 Hz irx carrier advantage: the large positive arc creates
a strong amplitude envelope across memories, and the single negative arc provides
contrast. The irx phase relaxation then works on a phase space shaped by this
amplitude-differentiated landscape. At 1.0 Hz, the amplitude landscape oscillates
without strong net differentiation.
