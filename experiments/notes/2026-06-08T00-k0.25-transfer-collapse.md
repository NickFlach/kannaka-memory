## Hypothesis

The T11 discovery showed a super-additive carrier_e jump when K dropped from 1.0 to 0.5
at A=0.15 (0.584 → 0.853, +0.285). This effect was absent at A=0.10 (T14: 0.568 → 0.549,
a slight drop). The T14 notes flagged K=0.25 as an open question. Since K-sweeps prior to
T11 only tested K≥1.0 and K=0.5 was found by comparison, no trial at K=0.25+A=0.15 existed.

**Prediction:** K=0.25 + A=0.15 (code defaults for DRIVE_FREQ=0.5, DRIVE_SCOPE=all,
DREAM_MODE=stage_sync) continues the carrier_e trend upward, with xi broadly similar to
K=0.5 (0.873). Expected fitness < 0.104.

**Mechanistic basis:** weaker Kuramoto coupling leaves more phase diversity intact, allowing
the 0.5 Hz drive to stamp amplitude structure more coherently — the same mechanism behind
the T11 super-additive effect.

## Analysis

### Transfer collapse: structural, not stochastic

transfer_score fell from 0.655 (K=0.5, 3-trial avg) to 0.408 — a 38% drop well outside
the observed noise range (0.561–0.702 across 9 K=0.5 trials). Transfer measures primed-vs-naive
discrimination: how well a dream on corpus A helps retrieve B. At K=0.25, the Kuramoto coupling
is too weak to guide phases toward category attractors — the 0.5 Hz drive dominates phase
dynamics, creating amplitude-sorted phase clustering that is specific to corpus A and does not
generalize cross-corpus. At K=0.5, residual Kuramoto guidance imprints enough category-level
structure to preserve transfer. K=0.25 falls below this guidance threshold.

Fitness arithmetic for the transfer collapse:
- (1 − 0.408) × 0.15 = 0.089 vs (1 − 0.655) × 0.15 = 0.052 at K=0.5 → +0.037 fitness cost

### Xi spike: genuinely high, but cannot compensate

xi=0.990 is the highest single-trial xi recorded in any stage_sync run. Adversarial robustness
is excellent at K=0.25 — consistent with maximum phase diversity leaving no exploitable
adversarial gap. But:
- (1 − 0.990) × 0.15 = 0.0015 vs (1 − 0.873) × 0.15 = 0.019 at K=0.5 → −0.018 fitness benefit

Net: transfer costs +0.037, xi saves −0.018, net regression ≈ +0.019. Observed: +0.026.
Remaining gap (~+0.007) likely from other metrics affected by the regime change.

### Non-monotone R-K relationship: new data point

The R-K history across all stage_sync trials:
- K=5.0 (T24): R ≈ 0.409
- K=3.0 (T24): R ≈ 0.362
- K=1.0 (T00, T14): R ≈ 0.250
- K=0.5 (T14): R = 0.161
- **K=0.25 (this fire): R = 0.390**

R is NOT monotone in K. It has a minimum near K=0.5 and rises both for K>>1 (strong coupling
synchronizes phases → high R) and for K<<0.5 (drive-dominated dynamics create drive-frequency
phase clusters → also high R). Two qualitatively different mechanisms produce high R:

1. **High K (≥1.0):** Kuramoto coupling synchronizes phases within categories → moderate R
2. **Low K (≤0.25):** Drive dominates, creating amplitude-selected phase clusters tied to the
   drive frequency → R rises via a different mechanism, but transfer collapses

This means R is not a reliable indicator of "healthy" phase structure — R=0.390 can mean
either (1) strong Kuramoto-guided category sync or (2) drive-dominated collapse of transfer
generalization. The magic-gravity hypothesis (high R → high query_gravity) does NOT hold:
R=0.390 gives query_gravity=0.448 (below 0.5 threshold).

### K=0.5 is the stable lower boundary for cross-corpus transfer

K=0.5 preserves both strong carrier_e (0.853) and adequate transfer (0.655). K=0.25 breaks
transfer while improving xi. The coupling stability threshold for cross-corpus transfer appears
to lie between K=0.25 and K=0.5. K=0.5 is confirmed as the operating point that balances:
- Phase diversity (xi robustness)
- Category-guided structure (transfer generalization)
- Drive-induced carrier emergence

## Implications

1. **K=0.5 is not just "better than K=1.0" — it is at a stability boundary.** Below K=0.5,
   transfer collapses. The K-sweep has now bracketed the optimum: K={0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0,
   3.0, 5.0, 7.0} tested, and K=0.5 wins on fitness. No further K exploration warranted.

2. **R-K relationship is non-monotone.** High R is produced by two mechanistically distinct
   regimes (strong coupling sync vs drive-dominated clustering). R alone cannot distinguish
   healthy from pathological phase structure. The magic-proxy interpretation of R requires a
   qualifier: R must be elevated by category-level Kuramoto guidance, not by drive-frequency
   locking.

3. **Query_gravity does not track R.** R=0.390 at K=0.25 produces query_gravity=0.448
   (below 0.5), virtually identical to K=0.5 (0.161 R, 0.446 gravity). The gravity effect
   depends on something other than global phase concentration.

4. **Xi=0.990 at K=0.25 is the highest single stage_sync xi observed.** This confirms
   the phase-diversity → xi robustness mechanism holds even below the transfer stability
   threshold. Future work on xi could explore decoupling xi from transfer — though fitness
   weights make this difficult without a transfer-preserving mechanism for high xi.
