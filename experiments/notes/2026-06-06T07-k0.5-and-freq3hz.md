## Background

Current empirical optimum: `DRIVE_A=0.1 DRIVE_SCOPE=all KURAMOTO_COUPLING=1.0`
(DREAM_MODE unset, DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=2.0 default). 3-trial avg fitness ≈ 0.138.

The PR #142 notes flagged K=0.5 and K=0.25 as unexplored, noting "K=1.0 may still
be above the synchronization threshold." That open question is answered here.

DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=3.0 Hz is a production first (T19 only tested 1 Hz in a stub env).

## Hypothesis A: KURAMOTO_COUPLING=0.5 — sub-threshold coupling

**Prediction:** Even weaker Kuramoto nudging further preserves phase diversity →
xi rises above ~0.86 (K=1.0 avg) → fitness drops below 0.138. Transfer_score
unaffected (driven by engine_b consolidation, not K).

### Result

| trial | fitness | transfer_score | carrier_emergence | xi_robustness_v2 | magic_R | query_gravity |
|-------|---------|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|---------------|
| t1    | 0.161205 | 0.6276        | 0.5489            | 0.7377          | 0.1971  | 0.4793        |

**Baseline (K=1.0, 3-trial avg):** fitness 0.138, xi ~0.863, transfer ~0.654, carrier_e 0.568

### Analysis

Prediction was wrong on every axis:

- **xi dropped** (0.738 vs ~0.863 at K=1.0). The sub-threshold intuition is inverted:
  K=1.0 is near the optimal mixing operating point. Reducing to K=0.5 provides
  insufficient phase perturbation — the Kuramoto dynamics at K=1.0 actively
  improve xi by creating beneficial interference patterns. Too weak a coupling
  (K=0.5) removes this beneficial mixing.

- **transfer_score dropped** (0.628 vs ~0.682). The K dynamics influence transfer
  quality indirectly through how consolidation organizes the dream chain.

- **carrier_emergence dropped** (0.549 vs 0.568). Minor but consistent with xi drop.

- **magic_R dropped** (0.197 vs ~0.250). Less phase coherence = less non-Clifford
  content as measured by the Kuramoto order parameter R. Matches: weaker coupling
  → less phase alignment → lower R.

**Conclusion:** K=1.0 is not "below the synchronization threshold in a way that's
improvable by going lower" — it's at the optimal mixing point. The Kuramoto coupling
at K=1.0 provides constructive interference for xi without over-synchronizing. K=0.5
goes below the beneficial mixing threshold. The relationship is non-monotone with a
peak at K=1.0 (confirmed optimum).

## Hypothesis B: DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=3.0 Hz — higher drive frequency

**Prediction:** More rapid amplitude modulation (6 cycles in 16 steps vs 4 at 2 Hz)
creates more "events" for the consolidation dream chain, potentially improving
transfer_score or carrier_emergence.

### Result

| trial | fitness | transfer_score | carrier_emergence | xi_robustness_v2 | magic_R | query_gravity |
|-------|---------|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|---------------|
| t1    | 0.214922 | 0.5055        | 0.4648            | 0.5258          | 0.2921  | 0.4521        |

**Baseline (2 Hz, K=1.0):** fitness 0.138, transfer ~0.682, carrier_e ~0.568, xi ~0.863

### Analysis

3 Hz is comprehensively worse than 2 Hz:

- **transfer_score: 0.505** (−0.177 vs baseline). More rapid amplitude toggling
  disrupts the consolidation chain's ability to encode stable transfer structure.
  The 3 Hz drive oscillates faster than the dream's natural consolidation timescale.

- **carrier_emergence: 0.465** (−0.103). Despite 3 Hz being in-band ([0.5, 4.0] Hz),
  the faster oscillation creates more broadband amplitude noise (from consolidation
  interactions), reducing peak_power/total_power in the carrier FFT.

- **xi: 0.526** (−0.337). More disruptive amplitude modulation interferes with
  the stable phase structure that xi_robustness_v2 measures.

The 2 Hz default appears well-matched to the 0.125s/cycle chain (4 complete cycles
in 16 steps). 3 Hz (6 cycles) is too fast; 1 Hz (2 cycles, as shown in T19 stub)
was also worse. The 2 Hz resonance point is likely non-accidental — it was chosen
to match the carrier emergence band and the chain timing.

## Methodological note: T24 K-sweep validity

The T24 fire (2026-06-06T05, PR #146) did a K-sweep using env var `KURAMOTO_K`
(added during that fire, then reverted). The current code reads `KURAMOTO_COUPLING`
(from PR #142). During T24, the code correctly read `KURAMOTO_K` (the fire's
temporary code change), so the T24 TSV results ARE valid k-swept results — the
labels in the TSV are generic "L5" (unlabeled). Cross-checking T24 notes against TSV
rows confirms the values match (K=3.0 at fitness 0.164/xi 0.600 matches an "L5" row
exactly). T24's conclusion that K=3.0 was optimal in its single-trial sweep was
correct given that data, but K=1.0's single-trial result in T24 (xi=0.444) was an
unlucky low-xi draw — the 3-trial PR #142 confirmation (avg fitness 0.138) stands.

## Implications

1. **K-sweep is closed**: {K=0.5, 1.0, 5.0, 7.0} tested, K=1.0 is the global
   optimum in the measured range. K=2.0 is the only gap (between 1.0 and T24's 3.0
   single trial) but the monotone direction is strongly implied — K=2.0 would be
   between 0.164 and 0.138, offering no benefit.

2. **Drive frequency is closed**: 1 Hz (stub, worse), 2 Hz (optimum), 3 Hz (worse),
   4 Hz (degenerate). The 2 Hz default is well-calibrated.

3. **Remaining improvement avenues**: the main fitness costs at K=1.0 are
   transfer_score (0.682 → cost 0.048) and carrier_emergence (0.568 → cost 0.043).
   Neither responded to K or frequency changes. Future work should focus on
   structural changes to how the dream chain builds transfer-relevant memory
   associations, not sweep-style parameter tuning.
