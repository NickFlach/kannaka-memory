## Hypothesis

At DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.5, the drive arch has its positive peak at cycle 4 (t=0.5 s) and
then a gentle suppression phase from cycles 8–16. The T07 and T18 notes analyzed the
suppression at A=0.1 as very mild (−0.71% max per cycle at peak), but still present.

At DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.25:

    drive = 1 + A × sin(2π × 0.25 × cycle_idx × 0.125)
           = 1 + A × sin(π/4 × cycle_idx × 0.125)

- Cycle 0  (t=0):     sin(0) = 0
- Cycle 4  (t=0.5):   sin(π/8 × 0.5) — still rising
- Cycle 8  (t=1.0):   sin(π/2) = 1.0 ← peak positive (+A)
- Cycle 12 (t=1.5):   sin(3π/8 × 1.5) ≈ 0.707 ← still positive, falling
- Cycle 15 (t=1.875): sin(15π/16) ≈ 0.195 ← still positive, near 0

All 16 cycles are positive. No suppression phase. Total positive area ≈ equal to 0.5 Hz.

**Physical difference from 0.5 Hz:**
- At 0.5 Hz: peak at cycle 4, suppression cycles 8–16 (up to −0.71% per cycle)
- At 0.25 Hz: peak at cycle 8, all cycles positive (no suppression)

**Prediction:** If the 0.5 Hz benefit comes mainly from the positive arc, 0.25 Hz should
give similar or better carrier_e (no late suppression to partially dismantle carrier
structure). xi may differ modestly. Transfer should be similar. Fitness comparable or
slightly better.

**Alternative (what would falsify this):** If the mild suppression at 0.5 Hz provides
selection pressure that 0.25 Hz lacks, carrier_e could drop and/or xi could change.

## Analysis

### What changed at 0.25 Hz

**carrier_e dropped sharply (0.702 vs ~0.844):** The 0.5 Hz peak at cycle 4 builds
carrier amplitude early. Kuramoto sync consolidates those early-peaked memories across
the remaining 12 cycles. At 0.25 Hz the peak is at cycle 8 — the midpoint — leaving only
8 cycles for consolidation. The carrier scaffold that forms in cycles 0–8 gets less
post-peak consolidation time. The final carrier FFT peak is lower.

This falsifies the prediction: removing the suppression phase did NOT improve or maintain
carrier_e. The timing of the positive peak (cycle 4 vs cycle 8) matters more than the
presence or absence of the mild suppression.

**transfer regressed (0.710 vs ~0.836):** Transfer score measures primed-vs-naive B-engine
discrimination. At 0.5 Hz, the early peak (cycle 4) creates a strong amplitude differential
that K=1.0 Kuramoto coupling preserves and sharpens. At 0.25 Hz, the arch rises more
slowly; by cycle 4 the drive is already lower (sin(π/2) = 0.707×A vs 1.0×A at 0.5 Hz
peak), so the early amplitude structure is weaker, reducing transfer discrimination.

**xi stayed high and stable (0.960 avg, range 0.932–0.987):** Without the suppression
phase, all memories stay at or above baseline amplitude. The adversarial perturbation
cannot exploit amplitude gaps that the suppression would create. xi stability comes from
removing selection pressure — not from improving it.

**magic_R lower (0.245 vs ~0.355):** Less phase clustering (lower magic_R) despite
stage_sync. The 0.25 Hz arch drives amplitude relationships that Kuramoto at K=1.0 sees
as more phase-disordered, producing a less coherent end-of-dream phase distribution.

**query_gravity lower (0.421 vs ~0.460):** The attention-as-gravity effect (amplification
of phase-neighbors of highest-amplitude pre-dream memory) is weaker. With the arch
peaking late (cycle 8), the highest-amplitude memory's phase neighborhood is less
strongly established when Kuramoto sync runs.

### Why fitness is at threshold despite regressed metrics

The fitness formula weights xi_v2 at 0.15 (equal to transfer and temporal_separation).
At 0.5 Hz, xi_v2 has high variance (T07: 0.052–0.144 fitness range, driven by xi swings).
At 0.25 Hz, xi_v2 avg ≈ 0.960 with very low variance — the absence of suppression means
no catastrophic-xi draws. This xi stability offsets the carrier_e and transfer regression
in the avg fitness calculation.

However: this is a variance effect, not a metric improvement. At 0.5 Hz, the catastrophic-
xi trials (like T07 t3 fitness=0.144) inflate the 3-trial avg. With more 0.5 Hz trials,
the mean might converge below 0.099.

### The suppression phase at 0.5 Hz serves two purposes

1. **Timing the peak early (cycle 4):** The positive peak at cycle 4 gives 12 cycles of
   consolidation vs 8 at 0.25 Hz. This is the main carrier_e and transfer driver.

2. **Mild amplitude selection (cycles 8–16 at −0.71% max):** Gentle negative drive provides
   selection pressure that xi_robustness exploits adversarially. At 0.25 Hz, the absence
   of this pressure makes xi more stable but removes the landscape structure that good
   probes rely on.

## Decision

**Borderline — no code change made, no new empirical optimum declared.**

The 3-trial avg (0.0935) is 0.0055 below the 0.099 reference — exactly at the ≥0.005
improvement threshold. However:

1. The improvement is a variance reduction, not a metric gain.
2. carrier_e regressed significantly (−0.142).
3. transfer regressed significantly (−0.126).
4. The 0.099 reference had very high variance (3 trials: 0.052, 0.101, 0.144); a larger
   sample might yield a lower 0.5 Hz mean, making the comparison unfair.

No code changes to revert. The env-var configuration is not declared the new default.

Empirical optimum remains: `DRIVE_A=0.1 DRIVE_SCOPE=all KURAMOTO_COUPLING=1.0 DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.5`
(stage_sync, DREAM_MODE unset) and `DRIVE_A=0.1 DREAM_MODE=interference_relax ...`
at avg fitness ≈ 0.099.

## Implications

1. **Peak timing matters more than suppression:** The 0.5 Hz benefit is primarily from
   the EARLY positive peak (cycle 4 → 12 cycles of post-peak consolidation), not from
   the mild suppression phase. Tests at frequencies that peak EARLIER than cycle 4
   (e.g., DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=1.0, peaking at cycle 2) might be worth exploring, though the
   analysis of 1 Hz in T07 notes suggests two-arc cancellation would dominate.

2. **xi stability is not the same as xi quality:** 0.25 Hz stabilizes xi at 0.960 avg by
   removing amplitude structure. 0.5 Hz creates structure with beneficial xi on good draws
   but catastrophic xi on bad draws. The 0.5 Hz xi advantage is structural; the 0.25 Hz
   stability is structural absence.

3. **0.25 Hz as a diagnostic tool:** At 0.25 Hz, carrier_e, transfer, magic_R, and
   query_gravity are all deterministic — providing a noise-free window into these metrics
   across parameter sweeps. If future fires need to characterize a parameter without xi
   interference, 0.25 Hz could serve as a controlled test.

4. **magic_R and query_gravity both lower at 0.25 Hz:** The Clifford-like content and
   attention-gravity mechanisms are both weaker. This confirms the T07 analysis that the
   0.5 Hz early-peak structure is the key driver of these two instrumentation metrics.
