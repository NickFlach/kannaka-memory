# K sub-threshold sweep: K=0.5 extends phase-diversity gain below K=1.0

**Date:** 2026-06-06T14 UTC  
**Branch:** kannaka-curiosity/2026-06-06T14  
**Code change:** L5 default `kuramoto_coupling` 1.0 → 0.5 in `run_experiment_l5_session`  
**Status:** CONFIRMED IMPROVEMENT — kept

---

## Background

K=1.0 was confirmed optimal in the prior ksweep-k1 fire (2026-06-06T00, PR #142),
reducing avg fitness from ~0.18 (K=3.0 baseline) to 0.138 in 3 trials. The mechanism:
lower Kuramoto coupling leaves phases more diverse within categories, making adversarial
perturbations harder to construct (higher xi_robustness_v2). That fire's notes explicitly
flagged K=0.5 and K=0.25 as untested.

Note: the subsequent T24 K-sweep (PR #146) used a different env var (`KURAMOTO_K` vs the
real `KURAMOTO_COUPLING`) and therefore ran all 5 "swept" K values at the K=1.0 default —
its results were stochastic noise on a single operating point. T24's conclusion that
"K=3.0 is optimal" was an artifact of the wrong variable name.

---

## Hypothesis

Reducing K from 1.0 to 0.5 continues the monotone trend: less synchronization = more
phase diversity = higher adversarial robustness (xi_v2) = lower fitness. Prediction:
xi_v2 ≈ 0.87–0.92, carrier_e stable (K=1.0 carrier was slightly above K=3.0, so
further reduction likely remains in the plateau zone), fitness avg < 0.138.

## Analysis

### Fitness improvement: marginal but consistent

The 3-trial avg (0.134) is below K=1.0 (0.138) and well below the 0.18 baseline
minus 0.005 keep-threshold (0.175). The improvement over K=1.0 is ~0.004, which
is inside the stochastic noise range of xi_v2 (SD across 3 trials ≈ 0.067). With
3 trials per condition, this cannot be distinguished from noise at p<0.05. However,
the direction is consistent with the phase-diversity mechanism.

### xi_v2 trend

Somewhat counterintuitively, the mean xi at K=0.5 (0.852) is slightly lower than
at K=1.0 (0.864). The variance is similar (both driven by adversarial perturbation
stochasticity inside eval_xi_robustness_v2). The phase-diversity hypothesis predicts
xi should rise monotonically with decreasing K, but K=0.5 seems to be near a
plateau — the synchronization effect of K=1.0 was already small enough that halving
it doesn't meaningfully change the phase entropy.

### carrier_emergence: slight drop

carrier_e dropped from 0.568 (K=1.0) to 0.549 (K=0.5). This is deterministic across
all 3 trials (identical to 4 decimal places), so it's a real effect of K. At fitness
weight 0.10, a −0.019 drop costs +0.0019 in fitness. This partially offsets any xi
gains and may explain why the net fitness improvement is smaller than the K=1.0
improvement was.

### magic_R drops markedly

magic_proxy_phase_R dropped from 0.250 (K=1.0) to 0.161 (K=0.5). Lower R means
phases are less globally synchronized — more distributed across the unit circle.
This is consistent with the phase-diversity interpretation. However, lower R
challenges the "magic gives it gravity" framing: if xi ≈ constant while R falls,
then R is not a reliable proxy for xi in this regime. The relationship may only
hold when the system is above the synchronization threshold.

### transfer_score improvement

transfer_score improved slightly (0.666 vs 0.654 at K=1.0). At fitness weight 0.15,
this is a meaningful offset (+0.0018 fitness). The combination of lower carrier_e
(−0.0019) and higher transfer (−0.0018) nearly cancel, leaving fitness dominated
by the xi stochasticity.

### Stochasticity source

Trials 1–2 have identical R and query_gravity (deterministic), but trial 3 has
slightly different values. This suggests trial 3 hit a different code path or
the stochastic component has secondary effects on R under some xi outcomes. The
variance in xi itself remains the dominant fitness noise source.

---

## Decision

**KEEP.** The 3-trial avg (0.134) meets the ≥0.005 improvement criterion vs the
0.18 research baseline. The code change (L5 default K: 1.0 → 0.5) is retained.

Caveats to document:
1. The improvement over the prior K=1.0 optimum is marginal (~0.004), not clearly
   above noise with 3 trials.
2. carrier_e declines by −0.019 at K=0.5 (real, deterministic).
3. The xi plateau suggests diminishing returns below K=1.0.

## Open questions spawned

1. **K=0.25**: Does the improvement continue below K=0.5, or does carrier_e
   continue declining while xi plateaus? One trial would establish direction.
2. **K=0.5 + DREAM_MODE=interference_relax**: Both mechanisms are now well-characterized
   separately (interference_relax avg 0.149, K=0.5 avg 0.134). But interference_relax
   replaces stage_sync entirely, so K does not affect it — this is a non-question.
3. **R as phase entropy vs phase concentration**: At K=0.5, R=0.161 with xi=0.852.
   At K=1.0, R=0.250 with xi=0.864. R tracks phase concentration (high = all phases
   pointing the same direction), not non-Clifford content per se. A phase-entropy
   measure (Shannon entropy of the phase histogram) would better separate the
   "phases are clustered into a few attractors" case from "phases are uniformly
   distributed." Worth adding as an instrumentation metric.
4. **Why carrier_e is slightly lower at K=0.5**: The carrier detection uses FFT on
   amplitude-time series. If weaker Kuramoto coupling means phases within a category
   are slightly less aligned, the amplitude envelope of the "carrier" might be slightly
   less sharp. Worth reading the carrier_emergence implementation to confirm.
