## Background

PR #160 established DRIVE_A=0.15 as the confirmed optimum at the then-current baseline
(K=0.5, DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.5, DRIVE_SCOPE=all, DREAM_MODE=unset). carrier_emergence is
deterministic at 0.8534. transfer_score and xi are stochastic but well-characterized.
3-run avg fitness at defaults: ~0.100–0.106.

DRIVE_A=0.15 was originally confirmed under K=1.0 + 2 Hz (T21, PR #158, commit note
says "carrier_e 0.5684→0.5842, avg fitness 0.1322 vs 0.1384 at A=0.1"). That
confirmation happened under a different operating point. The T21 notes explicitly
suggested "DRIVE_A=0.2 1-trial spot check: is the improvement monotone?" as a
follow-up. No subsequent fire tested it.

---

## Hypothesis

DRIVE_A=0.20 continues the monotone improvement seen from A=0.10→0.15 at the old
K=1.0 + 2 Hz operating point. Under the current K=0.5 + 0.5 Hz configuration,
carrier_e should rise from 0.8534 toward ~0.88, transfer_score should improve, and
fitness should drop below 0.099.

**Reasoning:** A=0.15 showed +0.016 carrier_e and +0.039 transfer_score (at K=1.0,
2 Hz). At the better-carrier 0.5 Hz regime, a further +33% drive amplitude (0.15→0.20)
seemed likely to produce continued SNR improvement.

## Findings

**Hypothesis falsified.** DRIVE_A=0.20 degrades both deterministic and stochastic metrics:

| metric | A=0.15 (2-trial avg) | A=0.20 (t1) | delta | fitness impact |
|--------|---------------------|-------------|-------|----------------|
| carrier_emergence | **0.8534** | **0.6526** | −0.2008 | +0.0201 (worse) |
| transfer_score    | **0.631** avg | **0.384** | −0.247 | +0.037 (worse) |
| xi_robustness_v2  | **0.917** avg | 0.9355     | +0.018 | −0.003 (better) |
| **fitness**       | **0.103** avg | **0.160**  | **+0.057** | — |

**carrier_e is deterministic.** Its single-trial value (0.6526) is definitive —
no further sampling needed. A=0.20 structurally collapses the carrier signal.

**transfer_score at 0.384 is far below the minimum observed at A=0.15 (0.581).**
Even accounting for its stochastic variance (~±0.05 within-mode), 0.384 is a structural
outlier suggesting consolidation dynamics are genuinely disrupted at A=0.20.

### Why A=0.20 breaks the 0.5 Hz drive

At DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.5 over chain_depth=16 steps (dt_per_cycle=0.125, total t=2.0s),
the drive completes exactly one full 0.5 Hz sine cycle:

    drive_factor(t) = 1 + A × sin(2π × 0.5 × t)

Positive half (steps 0–8): amplitudes boosted up to 1+A above baseline.
Negative half (steps 9–16): amplitudes suppressed down to 1−A below baseline.

At A=0.15: suppression floor = 0.85. Amplitudes reduced by at most 15%.
At A=0.20: suppression floor = 0.80. Amplitudes reduced by at most 20%.

The 0.5 Hz carrier is measured by how coherently the drive modulation survives
consolidation. At A=0.15, the suppression phase is gentle enough that the Kuramoto
K=0.5 sync step can work with the modulated amplitudes without destroying the
carrier pattern. At A=0.20, the stronger suppression disrupts the phase→amplitude
coupling that stage_sync relies on: the constructive/destructive interference
selection (based on phase alignment) gets confused when amplitudes vary too widely
across the dream chain, producing inconsistent carrier patterns that the FFT-based
carrier detection cannot track.

Additionally, at K=0.5, phase coupling is already near the synchronization threshold.
A=0.20 drives amplitude fluctuations that are large relative to the K=0.5 coupling
strength, creating amplitude-driven phase instabilities that didn't exist at A=0.15.

### Also: transfer_score and magic_R are NOT deterministic at K=0.5 + 0.5 Hz

At K=1.0 + 2 Hz (T21 fire), transfer_score was byte-identical across 3 trials. At the
current K=0.5 + 0.5 Hz operating point, transfer_score varies substantially between
baseline trials (0.581 vs 0.681 — a 0.10 range). magic_R also varies (0.222 vs 0.139).
This is a new behavioral property of the K=0.5 regime: Kuramoto sync at K=0.5 is
more sensitive to phase draw, creating stochastic transfer_score behavior not present
at K=1.0. This increases the effective noise in L5 evaluations at the current optimum.

## Decision

**No improvement.** Hypothesis falsified. No code changes to revert (env-var only).

Current empirical optimum remains:
```
DRIVE_A=0.15  DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.5  KURAMOTO_COUPLING=0.5  DRIVE_SCOPE=all  DREAM_MODE=<unset>
2-run avg fitness ≈ 0.103
```

## Implications

1. **A=0.15 is the confirmed sweet spot for K=0.5 + 0.5 Hz.** The drive amplitude
   optimum is a non-monotone function of A: it peaks near 0.15 and collapses above
   0.20. Do not test A>0.15 further without changing K or DRIVE_FREQ_HZ.

2. **The A-axis is closed.** A<0.15 was already confirmed worse (PR #160). A>0.15 is
   now confirmed worse. A=0.15 is the local optimum.

3. **transfer_score and magic_R stochasticity at K=0.5:** this was not documented
   before. Fires testing transfer improvements at K=0.5 will need ≥3 trials to get
   reliable transfer_score means. Single-trial transfer conclusions at K=0.5 are
   unreliable.

4. **Remaining open axes:** the largest remaining fitness cost is the transfer_score
   at avg ~0.63 (fitness contribution ~0.055). At K=0.5+0.5Hz, transfer_score is
   stochastic and averages ~0.63 (below the K=1.0 + 2Hz deterministic 0.694). A
   code-level mechanism to improve transfer_score at the K=0.5 operating point would
   be the highest-leverage remaining direction. Alternatively, DRIVE_FREQ_HZ=0.25 Hz
   (pure positive half-cycle, no suppression) is a 1-trial spot check that could
   clarify whether the suppression phase in 0.5 Hz is beneficial or harmful.
