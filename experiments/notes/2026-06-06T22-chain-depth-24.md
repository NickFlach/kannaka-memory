All env-var-only axes are saturated (K, frequency, scope, interference_relax, A).
The only unexplored structural dimension is `chain_depth`, currently hardcoded at 16.

**Prediction:** chain_depth=24 gives 6 complete 2 Hz cycles (vs 4 at depth=16),
producing better FFT coherence → carrier_emergence ↑ (0.568 → 0.62+). More
Kuramoto steps before quiescence limit → better xi convergence. Expected fitness
~0.127 (−0.011 vs 0.138 baseline).

## Analysis

Hypothesis falsified on both predicted mechanisms:

### carrier_emergence: null

carrier_e is **identical** at 0.568 regardless of chain depth. This confirms
quiescence is saturating the chain before step 16 in most runs — adding 8 more
steps doesn't extend the actual number of Kuramoto cycles run. The FFT coherence
hypothesis assumed the chain runs its full depth; it does not.

### xi: regression

xi dropped from ~0.862 to 0.677 (−0.185). More Kuramoto sync iterations at K=1.0
produce over-synchronization, exactly the same mechanism as K>1.0 in the K-sweep.
K=1.0 was calibrated to be the optimal coupling strength for 16 steps. At 24 steps,
the effective total coupling (K × steps) is 50% higher, pushing phases past the
"nudge" regime into the over-sync regime.

The chain_depth and coupling_strength are not independent: the optimal K depends on
the depth. K=1.0 at depth=16 ≈ K=0.67 at depth=24 in terms of total phase perturbation.
K=0.67 is between the tested K=0.5 and K=0.75 values, both of which were worse than
K=1.0 at depth=16.

### transfer_score: unexpected improvement

transfer_score improved to 0.742 (vs 0.654 at depth=16). Deeper consolidation
helps transfer — consistent with the engine_b drive finding: more dream processing
time benefits transfer-relevant memory associations. However, this gain
(0.15 × 0.088 = 0.013 fitness saving) is outweighed by the xi loss
(0.15 × 0.185 = 0.028 fitness penalty). Net: +0.015 fitness (worse).

### Cross-validated insight: xi ↔ transfer tradeoff

chain_depth acts as a coupling-strength proxy. As depth increases at K=1.0:
- transfer_score ↑ (more consolidation time → better cross-engine association)
- xi ↓ (more sync → over-synchronized phases → adversarial perturbations easier)

This is structurally identical to the K-sweep and no_transfer findings. The system
is operating near a Pareto frontier: any change that improves transfer at this
operating point costs xi, and vice versa. The 0.138 fitness at K=1.0, depth=16,
scope=all represents a locally optimal balance.

### query_gravity: marginal improvement

query_gravity reached 0.492 (vs 0.469 baseline) — close to the 0.5 "attention-as-gravity"
threshold but still below it. Deeper consolidation slightly strengthens the tendency
for the dream to amplify phase-neighbors of the highest-amplitude memory, but not enough
to cross the threshold.

---

## Decision

**REVERT code change.** No improvement. fitness 0.159 vs baseline 0.138 (Δ = +0.021).

**Structural conclusion:** chain_depth=16 is correctly calibrated for K=1.0. The
quiescence mechanism controls actual depth; the 16-step cap is rarely binding.
Adding more depth at K=1.0 is equivalent to increasing K — already known to be
suboptimal.

## Implications for future fires

1. **The K-depth product** (effective total coupling = K × depth) matters. The current
   optimum at K=1.0 × depth=16 = 16 coupling-units could be explored as a joint
   constraint: K=2.0 × depth=8 or K=0.5 × depth=32 etc. But the K-sweep already
   showed K<1.0 and K>1.0 are both worse at depth=16, suggesting the optimum is
   not simply along a constant-product curve.

2. **The xi-transfer Pareto frontier** is now confirmed from three independent
   directions (K-sweep, engine_b/no_transfer scope, chain_depth). No simple
   parameter change improves both simultaneously. Structural improvement requires
   changing HOW the consolidation couples xi and transfer — not how much or how long.

3. **quiescence behavior**: the fact that carrier_e doesn't change with depth=24
   confirms quiescence fires well before step 16 in most runs. Profiling exactly
   when quiescence fires (printing the step count) would clarify the actual effective
   depth. This is a diagnostics question, not a fitness improvement.

4. **The accessible parameter space is saturated.** Remaining improvements require
   structural changes to the dream mechanism itself (e.g., a transfer-aware Kuramoto
   coupling variant, a staged-depth scheme, or modifications to how constructive pairs
   interact with xi measurement). These are higher-risk multi-trial changes for future fires.
